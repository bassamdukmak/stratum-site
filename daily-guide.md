# How to read your Daily

Find a section below, then match each label to the field in your report.

Section names and fields can vary by edition and plan. Use the labels actually shown in your email. A Daily report can discuss a thesis lasting months: “Daily” describes the publishing cadence, not how long an idea lasts.

## Executive snapshot

The big picture before the individual ideas.

### Market regime

The edition’s description of the broader market environment: for example, whether investors are favoring risk or being more cautious. It is context, not a prediction of every stock’s direction.

### Top macro driver

The broad economic development receiving the most emphasis, such as rates, inflation or demand.

### Top geopolitical signal

An international development that may matter to markets. Read the stated connection rather than assuming every political headline affects your holdings.

### Top policy catalyst

A government, regulatory or central-bank development that may change the market backdrop.

### Bottom Line

The short synthesis of the edition. Start here for orientation, then read the supporting sections.

## Top five actionable ideas

Each idea has context, a research view and fields that help you interpret it.

### Ticker / Tickers

The exchange symbol used to identify a stock or fund. Look for the ticker field near the top of the idea. For example, AAPL identifies Apple; that example is not a recommendation. Check the instrument and exchange before using a symbol.

### Bullish / Bearish / Neutral

Bullish describes a favorable directional view; bearish an unfavorable one; neutral means no clear directional stance. Direction and today’s suggested posture are different: an interesting bullish theme can still be a watch-only idea.

### Confidence

How firmly the report presents the research view. Strong, Moderate and Tentative indicate different degrees of conviction. A displayed score or percentage is **not a calibrated chance of profit**, a guarantee or a suggested portfolio weight.

### Horizon / Time Horizon

The period over which the thesis is expected to develop. A 3–6 month horizon means the idea is framed around that period; it does not mean “buy today and automatically sell six months later.” Your own investment horizon may be different.

### Exit / When to walk away

A condition for reassessing or leaving the thesis: it might be a change in the evidence, a price level or another condition stated in that edition. Read the actual condition. It is not an order placed for you, and a horizon ending is not automatically an exit.

### Signal Type

The kind of support behind the idea. **Reported Data Anchor** points to reported facts or data; **Market Signal** to market behavior; **Developing Signal** to an unfolding situation; **Weak Signal** to a less established indication. The label describes the evidence, not a promised outcome.

### Today’s Posture / Decision

The edition’s stance for today, rather than the entire future of the idea. Read the full sentence alongside the verdict. **Watch** or **Monitor** means follow the situation rather than treat it as an immediate entry. **Build Slowly** describes a gradual stance; **Avoid** means the report is not supporting an entry.

### “Ready,” “wait” or watch-list wording

Read readiness together with any stated condition. Being ready to watch or being on a watch list is not the same as a buy signal. If the edition is waiting for confirmation, that condition still matters.

### What’s happening / Who benefits

The explanation of the idea and the companies or instruments linked to it. A company being mentioned as a beneficiary is not, on its own, a separate recommendation.

Illustrative reading example · Not a live idea

**Horizon: 3–6 months · Today’s posture: Monitor · Exit: Reassess if the stated catalyst fails.**

Read together: the thesis may take months, the current stance is to monitor, and the thesis needs reassessment if that condition occurs. It does not mean “buy now and hold for six months regardless.”

## Regional Radar

Understand the regional story and its possible market connection.

### Wildcard Signal

A signal from a region selected because it matters to that edition. “Wildcard” refers to flexible regional coverage, not a random stock, a lottery-like bet or an automatic high-risk trade.

### Story / Source

What happened and where the report attributes the information. Use the date and source link, when provided, to understand the context.

### Tickers to watch

Stocks or funds connected to the story. They help identify possible market exposure; inclusion is not an instruction to buy every ticker.

### Portfolio impact

The report’s general interpretation of how the development could affect relevant exposures. It does not mean Stratum has inspected your personal portfolio.

### Net Read

The short overall market takeaway from the story. For example, “risk-off catalyst” means a development that could encourage caution. “Net” means the overall interpretation, not net income or a trading profit.

### Connects To

The wider theme the story relates to, such as energy or interest rates. “Standalone macro signal” means it is presented as a separate broad-market development.

### Proxy Quality

How directly the listed instrument represents the story. A **Direct proxy** has a closer link; a **Sector proxy** captures the industry more broadly; a **Macro proxy only** is a broad economic exposure. **No clean US-listed proxy** means there is no clear instrument in that listing universe. Direct does not mean safe, certain or guaranteed to track the event.

### Posture

The research stance toward the signal. Read **Watch**, **Build slowly** or **Avoid** as the edition’s stance, alongside its explanation.

### Western coverage

How the report describes the story’s coverage in Western media: Widely covered, Under-reported or Not covered yet. It is a coverage assessment at publication time, not proof that nobody else knows about the story or that it will move prices.

## Macro snapshot

The economic backdrop, rather than a list of stock recommendations.

### Commodities

The prices and context for materials such as oil or gold. A displayed change needs its stated comparison period; it is not the return on an associated stock.

### 52-week range

The low-to-high trading range over the stated year-long period. It shows historical context, not a guaranteed floor or ceiling.

### Inflation pressure

A summary label such as Cooling, Stable, Mixed or Heating. It describes the report’s inflation read; it is not automatically a new official CPI release. Read any release date and number separately.

### Rates / Treasury yields

The interest-rate backdrop and yields on government debt, where shown. A 2-year or 10-year label refers to the bond’s maturity, not a stock idea’s horizon.

### FOMC / Next Fed decision

The Federal Reserve’s policy committee and its next scheduled decision, when listed. A calendar event does not tell you in advance what decision will be made.

### Hawkish / Dovish

Hawkish generally means a preference for tighter monetary policy; dovish for easier policy. These describe policy tone, not a guaranteed market reaction.

## Financial research / FA Deep Dive

The company-level financial context available with the relevant plan. “FA” does not mean you have a personal financial advisor.

### Current price / Data date

A quoted price at the report’s stated reference time. It may differ from the price available when you read the email.

### P/E and EPS

P/E compares share price with earnings per share. EPS is profit expressed per share. Compare like periods; a low P/E alone does not establish a bargain. N/M means not meaningful, often because earnings are zero or negative.

### TTM

Trailing twelve months: a measure covering the latest twelve-month period, rather than a forecast of the next year.

### Analyst target / Consensus

An estimate or opinion from analysts. Displayed upside or downside is the difference from the reference price—not a promised return or Stratum’s guarantee.

### Margins / ROE

Margins describe how much of revenue remains as profit at a specified level. ROE is return on shareholders’ equity. Read the period and business context; one high metric is not a complete assessment.

### Free cash flow / Debt-to-equity

Free cash flow describes cash remaining after the capital spending used in that measure. Debt-to-equity puts debt alongside shareholders’ equity. Definitions and comparisons matter across companies and industries.

### Dividend yield

The dividend expressed relative to share price. It is not total return, and dividends can change.

### Quant score / Data Grade / Verdict

Where present, the score is a summary research assessment; Data Grade describes the data-quality assessment, not whether a stock is safe. The verdict states the financial research conclusion. None of these removes the need to read the supporting information.

## Congressional Trading Watch

Available with Quant. Public information, with disclosure limits.

### Congressional Trading Watch

A section about public disclosures of US congressional transactions. It is not a real-time feed of a person’s account.

### Trade date / Filing date

The transaction date and the disclosure date are different. A newly filed report can describe an older trade.

### Value range / Buyer or seller

A disclosed amount may be a range rather than an exact sum. A transaction may concern a spouse or dependent where identified. Use the owner stated in the report.

### Connection to today’s themes

Context linking a disclosed trade to a wider market topic. Timing or theme overlap does not establish motive, illegal conduct or a reason to copy the trade.

## Put the fields together.

Start with the context, read the explanation, then check the posture, horizon and exit. No single label is a substitute for the rest of the report or for advice suited to your circumstances.
