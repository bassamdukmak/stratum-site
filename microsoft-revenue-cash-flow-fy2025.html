Microsoft’s revenue grew. Cash after investment fell.

Revenue growth and cash generation can tell different stories. Microsoft’s FY2025 results show why the headline needs a second look.

Analysis by Stratum Wealth · October 4, 2026
Historical comparison: fiscal years ended June 30, 2025 and 2024.

The figures behind the difference

Revenue rose 14.9%. Operating cash also increased, but cash equipment spending rose by more. The table separates reported figures from our calculation. PPE means property and equipment.

The cash bridge

Operating cash increased by $17.614bn; cash property-and-equipment spending increased by $20.074bn. The difference reduced the calculated remainder by $2.460bn, or 3.3%.

What this helps you read

A growing business can require more investment before additional sales turn into cash available for other uses. Revenue, accounting profit and cash flow answer different questions.

The bridge explains the arithmetic. It does not tell us whether the investment will pay off, establish the return on a particular project, or show that Microsoft’s shares are cheap or expensive. It also does not attribute all equipment spending to AI.

What the calculation leaves out

We define the simple free-cash-flow measure here as operating cash flow minus cash additions to property and equipment. It is our calculation, not a reported GAAP line item.

It excludes acquisitions, other investing flows and financing uses. Cash PPE additions do not include noncash finance-lease asset additions; finance-lease principal payments sit in financing flows. This is not a complete measure of infrastructure commitments or shareholder distributions.

These are historical annual results, not a current price assessment or trading recommendation.

Read a report with that distinction in mind

When a report discusses growth, ask which measure changed and which period it covers. Compare the supporting figures before drawing a conclusion from a headline.
